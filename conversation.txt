1. The Core Idea in Simple Terms
The Problem: "The Game of Telephone" in Marketing
When a business launches a product campaign, content is created by multiple people: copywriters write captions, designers build banners, and agencies draft PDF product sheets.   
PDF

Because everyone works separately, contradictions frequently slip through:   
PDF

The Instagram caption says 20% off, but the image banner says 30% off.   
PDF

The PDF product sheet says "keeps drinks cold for up to 12 hours," but the ad claim promises 24 hours guaranteed.   
PDF

An old logo is used on one banner, or required disclaimers like "On selected items" are forgotten.   
PDF

Manual review currently relies on people re-reading scattered files and comparing them against chat threads or PDFs. Human reviewers easily miss cross-asset inconsistencies, and approval is usually just a casual "looks good" Slack message with no clear audit trail.   
PDF
+ 1

[ Traditional Fragmented Workflow ]
Caption Draft ──┐
Banner Image  ──┼─> Manual Human Review ─> Missed Contradictions ─> Misleading Ad Published
PDF Document  ──┘    (Scattered Chat/PDFs)   ("Looks good" on Slack)
The Solution: CampaignGuard
CampaignGuard acts like an automated "spell-checker for brand facts and campaign consistency" before anything is published.   
PDF

It takes a single, human-approved brief (the "source of truth"), scans all campaign assets (captions, images, and PDFs) together, detects contradictions, explains the exact mistake with evidence, and records who approved the corrected version.   
PDF

The 4 Checking Layers
During preflight inspection, the system processes content across four layers:   
PDF

Campaign Facts: Verifies that numerical offers, discount percentages, expiration dates, eligibility requirements, and product claims match the contract.   
PDF

Cross-Asset Consistency: Compares the caption, image text, and PDF document against each other to catch internal disagreements.   
PDF

Brand Rules: Flags prohibited terminology, missing required disclosures, tone deviations, and outdated logos.   
PDF

Accessibility Checks: Performs basic checks such as visual contrast levels on banners and image alt-text presence.   
PDF

Core Architectural Principle: Deterministic Facts vs. Bounded AI
A key design choice in CampaignGuard is the separation between Deterministic Exact Matches and Bounded AI Interpretations:   
PDF

                             Incoming Check Request
                                       │
                   ┌───────────────────┴───────────────────┐
                   ▼                                       ▼
       [ Exact Structured Facts ]              [ Qualitative Content ]
    (Discounts, Expiry, SKUs, Dates)            (Tone, Messaging, Ambiguity)
                   │                                       │
                   ▼                                       ▼
       SQL Relational Lookup                    LLM Adapter Engine
   (100% Deterministic & Exact)              (Pydantic Structured Output)
Deterministic Lookups for Hard Facts: Dates, numerical discounts, product names, and eligibility criteria are stored as structured fields in PostgreSQL. They are checked using relational database lookups and exact string/numerical matching—never routed through vector embeddings or RAG semantic search, avoiding hallucinated facts.   
PDF
+ 1

Bounded AI for Qualitative Interpretation: The LLM is restricted to tasks requiring human-like comprehension: analyzing brand tone, evaluating claim semantics, and verifying nuanced text relationships. The LLM returns structured JSON enforced by Pydantic schemas.   
PDF
+ 2

Version-Bound Approval Security Mechanics
To prevent unauthorized changes after approval, CampaignGuard enforces a version-bound lock:   
PDF
+ 1

  [ Campaign Contract v1 ] ──+── [ Asset Pack Rev 1.0 ] ──> Approved Status [Lock Hash: A7X9]
                                │
                        User edits caption text
                                │
                                ▼
                       [ Asset Pack Rev 1.1 ] ──> APPROVAL INVALIDATED ──> Triggers Re-Review
Cryptographic/Version Binding: Approval status is bound to the hash of the exact asset revision (Rev 1.0) and the active contract version (v1).   
PDF
+ 1

Automatic Invalidation: Modifying any asset text, swapping an image, or updating the contract automatically resets the campaign status to Draft / Needs Re-Review.   
PDF
+ 1

Audit Trail: Every approval stores a permanent review record containing:

Approved content revision IDs.   
PDF

Contract and rule versions used.   
PDF

Completed vs. incomplete checks.   
PDF

Reviewer identity, timestamp, and exception override reasons.   
PDF

Security, Safety & Failure Modes
Prompt Injection Defenses: Uploaded PDFs, captions, and extracted OCR text are treated strictly as untrusted data payloads. They are injected into model prompts inside isolated data blocks, preventing malicious inputs from hijacking system instructions.   
PDF
+ 1

Explicit Failure State Handling:

Unreadable OCR / Blurry Text: System returns OCR_FAILED 
→ flags asset for manual human review.   
PDF

Model Service Unavailable: System returns SEMANTIC_CHECK_INCOMPLETE 
→ blocks final approval until resolved.   
PDF

Missing Evidence: System marks claim state as INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE 
→ prevents the AI from fabricating missing facts.   
PDF
